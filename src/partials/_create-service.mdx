import * as C from "@constants";
import { Callout } from "@stainless-api/docs/components";
import { NumberedList, NumberedItem } from "@components/NumberedList";

<NumberedList>

<NumberedItem title={`In ${C.CONSOLE}, click \`New service\`.`}>

This opens a three-step wizard: `Compute & Region`, `Environment`, then `Name & Create`. A cost
panel on the right shows the hourly and estimated monthly price, and updates as you change your
selections.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title="Choose your compute and region, then click `Continue`.">

Under `Compute`, select the CPU and memory your workload needs. `Shared` is the free option; the
rest are priced per hour. Under `Provider and region`, click the `AWS` or `Azure` tab, then choose a
region. Price varies by region, so the cost panel updates when you switch. See
[supported regions](/get-started/supported-regions) for the full list.

Every {C.SERVICE_SHORT} includes {C.TIMESCALE_DB} and the AI extensions. To create a
{C.SERVICE_SHORT} without them, click `Create with plain PostgreSQL`.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title="Configure your environment, then click `Continue`.">

Under `Environment type`, choose `Development` for ad hoc analytics, testing, and application
development, or `Production` for live workloads. `Production` defaults to settings optimized for
reliability and throughput.

Under `Add-ons`, you can also set:

- `High availability replicas`: replicas that take over from the primary if it crashes. Priced per
  replica. Defaults to `No replica (downtime)`.
- `Connection pooler`: PgBouncer, to manage connections efficiently. Off by default. See
  [connection pooling](/deploy/tiger-cloud/tiger-cloud-aws/service-management/connection-pooling).
- `VPC peering`: connect from your own cloud {C.VPC} without traffic leaving the private network.
  Defaults to `Don't connect my service with a VPC`.
- `Private Endpoints`: connect from your own virtual network without sending traffic over the public
  internet. Only endpoints in the region you chose are offered.

You can change all of these after the {C.SERVICE_SHORT} is created.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title="Name your service, then click `Create service`.">

{C.CONSOLE} suggests a name you can keep, edit, or regenerate. `Configuration summary` lists the
compute, region, environment, replica, and pooler settings you chose, so you can check them before
you commit. To change one, click `Back`. You can rename the {C.SERVICE_SHORT} later.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title={`Under \`Download your database config\`, click \`Download the config\` and store the configuration information you need to connect to this ${C.SERVICE_SHORT} in a secure location.`}>

{C.CONSOLE} shows a `Save the config` screen as soon as the {C.SERVICE_SHORT} is created. It offers
two files, each behind its own `Download the config` button: `Download your database config`, a
`.txt` file, and `Download Python .env file`, a `.env` file for a Python application. Either file
contains the passwords and configuration information you need to connect to your {C.SERVICE_SHORT}
using the {C.CONSOLE} {C.DATA_MODE}, from the command line, or using third-party database
administration tools. The same screen shows the connection string, which you can copy instead.

<Callout variant="important">

This is the only time you can access your {C.SERVICE_SHORT}'s password. Download one of the files or
copy the connection string before you leave this screen.

</Callout>

</NumberedItem>

</NumberedList>

You can [manage, pause, or delete](/deploy/tiger-cloud/tiger-cloud-aws/service-management/service-management) your {C.SERVICE_SHORT} at any time from {C.CONSOLE}.
