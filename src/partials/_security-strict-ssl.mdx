The default connection string for {C.CLOUD_LONG} uses the Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) mode `require`.
Users can choose not to use Transport Layer Security (TLS) while connecting to their databases, but connecting to
production databases without encryption is strongly discouraged. To
achieve even stronger security, clients may select to verify the identity of the
server. If you want your connection client to verify the server's identity, you
can connect with an [SSL mode](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/libpq-ssl.html#LIBPQ-SSL-SSLMODE-STATEMENTS) of `verify-ca` or `verify-full`. To
do so, your connection tool needs a trusted copy of the certificate chain: either
the operating system's trust store, or a file that you generate and store where
the tool can find it.

This section provides instructions for setting up a stricter SSL connection.

## SSL certificates

As part of the secure connection protocol, the server proves its identity by
providing clients with a certificate. This certificate should be issued and
signed by a well-known and trusted Certificate Authority.

Because requesting a certificate from a Certificate Authority takes some time,
{C.SERVICE_LONG}s are initialized with a self-signed certificate. This
lets you start up a {C.SERVICE_SHORT} immediately. After your {C.SERVICE_SHORT} is started, a
signed certificate is requested behind the scenes. The new certificate is
usually received within 30 minutes. Your certificate is then replaced
with almost no interruption. Connections are reset, and most clients reconnect
automatically. Free services do not supply SSL certificates.

With the signed certificate, you can switch your connections to a stricter SSL
mode, such as `verify-ca` or `verify-full`.

For more information on the different SSL modes, see the [{C.PG} SSL mode
descriptions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/libpq-ssl.html#LIBPQ-SSL-SSLMODE-STATEMENTS).

## Connect to your database with a stricter SSL mode

If your client uses libpq 16 or later, `psql` included, you don't need a certificate file. Set `sslrootcert` to
`system` so that the client verifies the server against the operating system's trust store, which already holds
the root certificates of both certificate authorities that {C.CLOUD_LONG} uses. Replace `<HOST>` and `<PORT>` with
the host and port of your {C.SERVICE_SHORT}:

```shell
psql "postgres://tsdbadmin@<HOST>:<PORT>/tsdb?sslmode=verify-full&sslrootcert=system"
```

Other drivers have their own trust store settings; see the SSL documentation for your driver.

For older clients, generate the certificate chain yourself and store it where your connection tool looks for it:

1.  Generate a copy of your certificate chain and store it in the right location
1.  Change your {C.CLOUD_LONG} connection string

## Verify the certificate type used by your database

To check whether the certificate has been replaced yet, connect to your
database instance and inspect the returned certificate. We are using two
certificate providers - Google and ZeroSSL, that's why chances are you can have
a certificate issued by either of those CAs:

```shell
openssl s_client -showcerts -partial_chain -starttls postgres -connect <HOST>:<PORT> < /dev/null 2>/dev/null  | grep "Google\|ZeroSSL"
```
