In {C.CLOUD_LONG}, replicas are copies of the primary data instance in a {C.SERVICE_LONG}.
If your primary becomes unavailable, {C.CLOUD_LONG} automatically fails over to your HA replica.

The replication strategies offered by {C.CLOUD_LONG} are:

- [High Availability (HA) replicas](../high-availability/high-availability): significantly reduce the risk of downtime and data
  loss due to system failure, and enable {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s to avoid downtime during routine maintenance.

- [Read replicas](../high-availability/read-scaling): safely scale a {C.SERVICE_SHORT} to power your read-intensive
  apps and business intelligence tooling and remove the load from the primary data instance.

For {C.MST_SHORT}, see [Failover in {C.MST_LONG}](/deploy/mst/failover).
For {C.SELF_LONG}, see [Replication and high availability](/deploy/self-hosted/replication-and-ha/about-ha).

## Rapid recovery

By default, all {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s have rapid recovery enabled.

Because compute and storage are handled separately in {C.CLOUD_LONG}, {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s recover
quickly from compute failures, but usually need a full recovery from backup for storage failures.

- **Compute failure**: the most common cause of database failure. Compute failures
can be caused by hardware failing, or through things like unoptimized queries,
causing increased load that maxes out the CPU usage. In these cases, data on disk is unaffected
and only the compute and memory needs replacing. {C.CLOUD_LONG} recovery immediately provisions
new compute infrastructure for the {C.SERVICE_SHORT} and mounts the existing storage to the new node.
{C.PG} crash recovery then replays any unapplied WAL from disk. This process typically only takes thirty seconds. However,
depending on the amount of WAL that needs replaying this may take up to twenty minutes. Even in the
worst-case scenario, {C.CLOUD_LONG} recovery is an order of magnitude faster than a standard recovery
from backup.

- **Storage failure**: in the rare occurrence of disk failure, {C.CLOUD_LONG} automatically
[performs a full recovery from backup](../high-availability/backup-restore).

If CPU usage for a {C.SERVICE_SHORT} runs high for long periods of time, issues such as WAL archiving getting queued
behind other processes can occur. This can cause a failure and could result in a larger data loss.
To avoid data loss, {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s are monitored for this kind of scenario.
