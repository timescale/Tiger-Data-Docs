---
title: "Sizing hypertable chunks"
description: "Fit active chunks in memory, balance chunk count vs compression, and align chunk_time_interval with shared_buffers on Tiger Cloud."
---

Chunks should be **small enough** that active data can be cached for fast access, but **large enough** that you do not create so many chunks that planning and compression suffer.

## Active vs inactive chunks

- **Active** chunks receive new writes (and their indexes) and should fit comfortably in cache.
- **Inactive** chunks are older and mostly read-only; they can rely more on disk.

## shared_buffers on Tiger Cloud

On Tiger Cloud, `shared_buffers` is commonly **25%** of service RAM. Size `chunk_time_interval` so the working set of **active** chunks (including indexes) stays within what you can reasonably expect to cache, for example, on a 64&nbsp;GB service, think in terms of on the order of 16&nbsp;GB for shared buffers, and keep active chunk footprint aligned with your workload.
