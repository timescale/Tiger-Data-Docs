## Drop and chunk management

- Use [`DROP TABLE`](/hypertables/drop/) to remove a hypertable. Very large drops may need memory; if they time out, [`drop_chunks()`](/reference/timescaledb/hypertable/drop_chunks/) first, then drop the table.
- You can [change chunk time intervals](/hypertables/change-chunk-intervals/) for new chunks; existing chunks keep their interval until reorganized.

## Schema changes

[`ALTER`](/hypertables/alter/) on a hypertable works like a normal {C.PG} table. If **compression** is enabled, adding columns with **defaults or constraints** may require decompressing affected data first.

## Vacuum and bloat

Heavy `INSERT`/`UPDATE`/`DELETE` causes bloat. [Autovacuum](https://www.timescale.com/learn/how-to-reduce-bloat-in-large-postgresql-tables) runs by default; tune it for your write rate. See also this [EDB article on VACUUM and ANALYZE](https://www.enterprisedb.com/blog/postgresql-vacuum-and-analyze-best-practice-tips-0). Use [`VACUUM ANALYZE`](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-vacuum.html) when you need to refresh statistics or clean up bloat.

## Deleting data

Prefer [`drop_chunks()`](/reference/timescaledb/hypertable/drop_chunks/) to remove whole time ranges instead of massive `DELETE` when possible. For policy-driven deletion, use [data retention](/reference/timescaledb/data-retention/).

## Observability

Use the service explorer and [informational views](/reference/timescaledb/informational-views/) to inspect hypertables from SQL.
