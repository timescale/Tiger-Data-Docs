[AWS Transit Gateway](https://aws.amazon.com/transit-gateway/) enables you to securely connect to your **{C.CLOUD_LONG} on AWS** from Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, AWS, or any other cloud or on-premise environment.

You use AWS Transit Gateway as a traffic controller for your network. Instead of setting up multiple direct connections to different clouds, on-premise data centers, and other AWS services, you connect everything to AWS Transit Gateway. This simplifies your network and makes it easier to manage and scale.

You can then create a peering connection between your {C.SERVICE_LONG}s and AWS Transit Gateway in {C.CLOUD_LONG}. This means that, no matter how big or complex your infrastructure is, you can connect securely to your {C.SERVICE_LONG}s.

For enhanced security, you can add peering connections to multiple Transit Gateways with overlapping CIDRs, {C.CLOUD_LONG} creates a new isolated connection for every unique Transit Gateway ID. Otherwise, the existing connection is reused for your {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s in the same project and region.

To configure this secure connection, you:

1. Connect your infrastructure to AWS Transit Gateway.
1. Create a {C.CLOUD_LONG} Peering {C.VPC} with a peering connection to AWS Transit Gateway.
1. Accept and configure the peering connection on your side.
1. Attach individual {C.SERVICE_SHORT}s to the Peering {C.VPC}.

AWS Transit Gateway enables you to connect from almost any environment, this page provides examples for the most common use cases.

<Tabs>

<TabItem label="Amazon Web Services">

<NumberedList>

<NumberedItem title={`Create a Peering ${C.VPC} in ${C.CONSOLE}`}>

1. In `Security` > `VPC`, click `Create a VPC`:

   <ThemeImage light={imgAddPeeringVpcTigerConsoleLight} dark={imgAddPeeringVpcTigerConsoleDark} alt="Creating a new peering VPC in Tiger Console" />

1. Choose your region and IP range, name your VPC, then click `Create VPC`:

   <ThemeImage light={imgConfigurePeeringVpcTigerConsoleLight} dark={imgConfigurePeeringVpcTigerConsoleDark} alt="Configuring VPC region, IP range, and name" />

   Your {C.SERVICE_SHORT} and Peering {C.VPC} must be in the same AWS region. The number of Peering {C.VPC}s you can create in your project depends on your [{C.PRICING_PLAN}](../../pricing-and-account-management). If you need another Peering {C.VPC}, either contact [support@tigerdata.com](mailto:support@tigerdata.com) or change your plan in [{C.CONSOLE}](https://console.cloud.tigerdata.com/).

1. In the `VPC Peering` column, click `Add`.

1. Provide your AWS account ID, Transit Gateway ID, CIDR ranges, and AWS region. {C.CLOUD_LONG} creates a new isolated connection for every unique Transit Gateway ID.

   <ThemeImage light={imgAddPeeringTigerConsoleLight} dark={imgAddPeeringTigerConsoleDark} alt="Adding a peering connection with AWS account details" />

1. Click `Add connection`.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title="Accept and configure peering connection in your AWS account">

Once your peering connection appears as `Processing`, you can accept and configure it in AWS:

1. Accept the peering request coming from {C.CLOUD_LONG}. The request can take up to 5 min to arrive. Within 5 more minutes after accepting, the peering should appear as `Connected` in {C.CONSOLE}.
1. Configure at least the following in your AWS account networking: your subnet route table to route traffic to your Transit Gateway for the Peering VPC CIDRs; your Transit Gateway route table to route traffic to the newly created Transit Gateway peering attachment for the Peering VPC CIDRs; and security groups to allow outbound TCP 5432.

</NumberedItem>

<NumberedItem title={`Attach a ${C.CLOUD_LONG} service to the Peering VPC in ${C.CONSOLE}`}>

1. Select the {C.SERVICE_SHORT} you want to connect to the Peering VPC.
1. Click `Operations` > `Security` > `VPC`.
1. Select the VPC, then click `Attach VPC`.

You cannot attach a {C.SERVICE_LONG} to multiple {C.CLOUD_LONG} {C.VPC}s at the same time.

</NumberedItem>

</NumberedList>

</TabItem>
