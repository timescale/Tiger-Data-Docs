## Overview

Rapid recovery in Tiger Cloud ensures your database can be restored quickly after failures, minimizing downtime and data loss. This guide covers disaster recovery strategies, recovery procedures, and how to build a resilient data infrastructure that can handle critical failures.

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of your Recovery Time Objective (RTO) and Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
- Configured backup strategy with appropriate retention
- High availability setup (recommended for production workloads)
- Documented disaster recovery runbook

## Understanding recovery objectives

### Recovery Time Objective (RTO)

How to determine the maximum acceptable downtime for your application and configure Tiger Cloud features to meet that target.

### Recovery Point Objective (RPO)

Understanding how much data loss is acceptable and selecting backup/replication strategies accordingly.

### RTO vs RPO trade-offs

The relationship between recovery speed, data loss tolerance, and infrastructure costs.

## Recovery scenarios

### Instance failure

How Tiger Cloud automatically detects and recovers from individual instance failures using standby replicas.

### Availability zone outage

Recovery procedures when an entire availability zone becomes unavailable.

### Regional disaster

Steps for recovering from a complete regional outage using cross-region backups.

### Data corruption

Recovering from logical data corruption using Point-in-Time Recovery (PITR) to restore to a known good state.

### Accidental deletion

Procedures for recovering accidentally deleted databases, tables, or data.

## Recovery methods

### Automatic failover

How Tiger Cloud's automatic failover promotes a standby replica to primary with minimal intervention.

### Manual failover

When and how to initiate a manual failover for planned maintenance or testing.

### Restore from backup

Step-by-step process for restoring a database from automated or on-demand backups.

### Point-in-Time Recovery

Using PITR to restore your database to a specific moment before an incident occurred.

## Building a disaster recovery plan

### Identify critical databases

Prioritizing which databases require the fastest recovery and most stringent RPO.

### Document recovery procedures

Creating runbooks that detail exact steps for each recovery scenario.

### Assign responsibilities

Defining roles and responsibilities for incident response and recovery operations.

### Communication plan

Establishing how to notify stakeholders during and after a recovery event.

## Testing your recovery plan

### Recovery drills

Scheduling regular failover and restore tests to validate your recovery procedures.

### Simulating failures

Using Tiger Cloud features to safely simulate various failure scenarios.

### Measuring actual RTO/RPO

Tracking recovery times during drills to ensure you meet your objectives.

### Post-drill review

Analyzing drill results and updating procedures based on lessons learned.

## Monitoring for rapid response

### Health monitoring

Configuring alerts for early detection of potential failures.

### Automated incident response

Setting up automation to initiate recovery procedures when failures are detected.

### Recovery progress tracking

Monitoring restoration progress and estimated time to completion.

## Best practices

- Document and regularly update your disaster recovery runbook
- Test recovery procedures at least quarterly
- Maintain backups in a geographically separate region
- Enable automatic failover for production databases
- Configure monitoring and alerting for rapid failure detection
- Keep recovery procedures simple enough to execute under pressure

Investigating factors that cause recovery to take longer than expected.

### Failover failures

### Data inconsistencies after recovery

Addressing scenarios where recovered data does not match expectations.

Resolving issues when the desired recovery point is unavailable.
